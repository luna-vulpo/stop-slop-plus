# Before / after pairs

Numbers match the pattern list in SKILL.md. Use them as calibration, not templates.

1. **Binary contrast.** "The question isn't the model. It's the eval." → "The eval matters more than the model."
2. **Negative listing.** "Not a tool. Not a platform. A teammate." → "It reviews your pull requests and leaves comments like a teammate would."
3. **Throat-clearing.** "Here's the thing: we shipped late." → "We shipped late."
4. **Faux insight.** "The part everyone misses: distribution is the real moat." → "Distribution is the moat."
5. **Colon reveal.** "The detail that makes it work: a separate agent grades it." → "A separate agent does the grading, which is what makes it work."
6. **Superficial -ing tail.** "The launch adds file search, highlighting the team's commitment to better workflows." → "The launch adds file search, so users can find old drafts without leaving the editor."
7. **Puffery.** "The launch marks a pivotal moment for the company." → "The launch is the company's first paid product."
8. **Metadiscourse.** "That last part matters more than it sounds. Retries are free." → "Retries are free, so a flaky test costs nothing to rerun."
9. **Weasel attribution.** "Studies show remote teams are more productive." → name the study, or ask the writer for it, or cut.
10. **Fake-strong verb.** "The app serves as a centralized hub for sponsor management." → "The app tracks sponsors, drafts, due dates, and approvals in one place."
11. **Synonym cycling.** "The agent reviews the draft. The assistant scores the piece. The tool suggests fixes." → "The agent reviews the draft, scores it, and suggests fixes."
12. **Dramatic fragmentation.** "We rewrote it. In Rust. In a weekend." → "We rewrote it in Rust over a weekend."
13. **Rhetorical scaffolding.** "What's the bottleneck? Reviews." → "Reviews are the bottleneck."
14. **Robotic rhythm.** Three paragraphs each opening with a one-line claim and closing with a one-line punch → vary length; merge where the thought continues.
15. **Fake-profound kicker.** "...and that's when I learned: code is a conversation." → delete; end on the previous concrete sentence.
16. **Summary ending.** "In conclusion, testing early saves time." (after a piece that showed exactly that) → delete.
17. **Decorative formatting.** "🚀 **Key takeaways**" over two bullets → one plain sentence.
18. **Em dash crutch.** "We tried it — and it worked — mostly." → "We tried it, and it mostly worked."
19. **Vague importance.** "The integration improved efficiency." → "The integration cut deploy time from 40 minutes to 4." (only if the number is in the source; otherwise "The integration shortened deploys." or ask)

## Polish

- „To nie jest narzędzie, to partner.” → „Narzędzie przegląda zmiany i zostawia uwagi.”
- „Warto zauważyć, że wdrożenie skróciło czas o połowę.” → „Wdrożenie skróciło czas o połowę.”
- „Kluczową rolę odgrywa tu komunikacja.” → „Zespół, który rozmawia codziennie, rzadziej powtarza pracę.” (tylko gdy źródło to mówi)
- „Dokonano analizy danych.” → „Przeanalizowaliśmy dane.”
- „Podsumowując, testy są ważne.” → usunąć.
